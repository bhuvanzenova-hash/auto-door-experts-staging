Automatic Doors AS5007 Compliance

26 Aug 2026 · 9 min read · The Auto Door Experts

Introduction

A community healthcare provider called us last month with a straightforward question: “Our sliding doors were installed over ten years ago—are they actually still legal?”

They genuinely weren’t sure. The doors opened when someone approached, closed after they walked through, and had rarely broken down. To anyone walking in off the street, the entrance looked perfectly fine. But when we arrived on site to run a standard safety audit, we found that the threshold presence sensors had drifted, the failsafe battery backup had degraded to zero holding charge, and the closing force exceeded safe limits for a pediatric or aged care environment.

It’s a far more common scenario than you might expect across Sydney and regional NSW. Automatic doors AS5007 compliance isn’t something most business owners or facility managers think about daily—until an annual safety audit, an insurance review, an NDIS accreditation inspection, or an unexpected safety incident forces the issue.

Here is the plain-English breakdown of what AS5007 requires, why it matters for your commercial property, and how to ensure your premises remain safe, accessible, and fully compliant without breaking the bank.

What Is AS5007?

AS5007 (specifically AS 5007:2007, Powered doors for pedestrian access and egress) is the governing Australian Standard dedicated to the design, installation, operation, and maintenance of pedestrian automatic doors.

Whether your building uses automatic sliding, swing, revolving, or telescopic doors, AS5007 establishes the minimum safety benchmarks required to prevent entrapment, impact injuries, and egress failures.

Core Areas Covered by AS5007

  • Sensor Coverage and Detection Zones: Outlines the mandatory width, depth, and positioning of microwave motion sensors (activation) and active infrared presence sensors (safety curtains) to ensure approaching pedestrians are detected well before reaching the threshold.
  • Kinetic Energy and Force Limits: Regulates how hard and how fast a powered door leaf is permitted to move. If a door makes contact with an obstruction—such as a person, pram, or mobility scooter—it must stop, reverse, or limit its force within strict milli-joule and Newton thresholds to avoid crush injuries.
  • Emergency Egress and Fail-Safe Functionality: Mandates how doors located along designated fire exit paths must behave during power outages or fire alarms (e.g., automatically driving open via monitored battery backups or allowing manual breakout).
  • Safety Signage and Visual Manifestations: Prescribes contrasting safety bands, directional indicators, and warning decals across clear glass panels to prevent accidental walk-ins.
  • Routine Maintenance Schedules: Specifies that commercial powered doors must be inspected, calibrated, and logged by qualified technicians at least once every six months.

Why Compliance Matters Beyond Ticking a Box

It is easy to view Australian Standards as administrative red tape, but non-compliant automatic doors represent a tangible physical hazard. Every week across Australia, poorly calibrated commercial doors cause preventable bruises, falls, and impact injuries—often involving young children, elderly visitors, or individuals with limited mobility.

From a commercial standpoint, non-compliance exposes property owners and facility managers to severe legal and financial risks:

1. Public Liability and Insurance Exposure

If a visitor or employee is injured by an automatic door that does not meet AS5007 specifications, standard public liability insurance policies may be contested or voided due to failure to maintain safe building equipment. An up-to-date maintenance logbook signed off by a qualified door technician is essential evidence of duty of care.

2. Work Health and Safety (WHS) Obligations

Under NSW Work Health and Safety legislation, building owners, commercial landlords, and business operators are legally obligated to maintain plant and mechanical equipment in a safe operating condition. Regular verification of automatic doors AS5007 compliance ensures your workplace meets statutory health and safety guidelines.

3. Protection of Vulnerable Building Users

For public-facing premises, safety standards ensure that the entrance accommodates users of all walking speeds. If a sensor detection zone is too shallow, an elderly patron using a walker may not trigger the door in time, or the door leaves might close prematurely while they are still crossing the threshold.

Automatic Doors and Disability Access

Automatic doors play an essential role in accessibility compliance, removing physical barriers for wheelchair and mobility scooter users, people with prams or trolleys, elderly customers, and people with temporary injuries.

Under the Disability Discrimination Act 1992 (DDA) and the Building Code of Australia (BCA) referenced through AS1428.1 (Design for access and mobility), commercial entrances must provide equitable, dignified access to all members of the public.

Essential Requirements for Disability Access Doors in Sydney

  • Clear Opening Widths: Disability access doors sydney commercial properties install must offer an unobstructed clear opening width (typically 850mm to 900mm minimum) to allow motorized wheelchairs and wide bariatric mobility chairs to pass comfortably without clipping the door frame.
  • Flush Thresholds: Bottom door guides and floor tracks must be recessed or fitted with compliant bevelled transitions. Any raised ridge or lip exceeding 3mm can catch wheelchair castors or create a trip hazard for individuals using walking frames.
  • Extended Hold-Open Times: Standard retail doors often close within 2 to 3 seconds. For accessible sites, door controllers must be calibrated to hold open longer, giving slower-moving individuals ample time to clear the threshold safety zone safely.
  • Accessible Push Pads & Touchless Actuators: If an automatic door relies on push buttons rather than motion sensors, the buttons must be installed at an accessible height (between 900mm and 1100mm from the finished floor level), clearly contrasted, and placed outside the swing path of the door.

Strict Compliance in Care Environments: NDIS, Healthcare & Aged Care

In specialized care sectors, compliant automated entry systems are fundamental to daily care, dignity, and infection control.

1. Automatic Doors for NDIS Providers

For registered National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) service providers, supported independent living (SIL) homes, and respite centers, automatic doors NDIS participants interact with must meet rigorous reliability standards. A door that requires heavy manual effort to push or one that closes abruptly compromises participant independence and fails NDIS practice standards.

2. Hospital Automatic Doors & Medical Centers

High-intensity healthcare facilities have unique mechanical demands. Hospital automatic doors in emergency departments, operating theaters, and sterile cleanrooms must combine rapid, reliable cycling with touchless, hygienic actuation. They must also interface directly with hospital building management systems (BMS) to switch between fire isolation modes, positive pressure airlock sealing, and emergency override states.

3. Aged Care Automatic Doors & Retirement Villages

In aged care and residential living communities, residents often move at slower speeds, experience reduced peripheral awareness, or use walking aids. Aged care automatic doors require wider infrared safety sensor curtains, lower motor driving forces, and soft-stop decelerations to eliminate any risk of door impact.

Retrofitting Older Doors to Meet Current Standards

Many older Sydney commercial buildings still have automatic doors installed years ago that predate current sensor and safety requirements. If your building’s entryway was installed before modern revisions of AS5007, it likely lacks the active presence detection curtains required today.

The good news is that achieving compliance does not always mean tearing out the entire shopfront.

In many cases, older doors can be retrofitted rather than fully replaced — upgrading the operator, sensors, and safety features at a fraction of the cost of full replacement.

What Can Be Retrofitted?

  • Modern Sensor Upgrades: Replacing obsolete radar motion units with combined motion-and-presence infrared sensors to ensure continuous coverage across the threshold.
  • Microprocessor Control Unit Swaps: Installing modern digital door controllers that feature intelligent obstruction detection and adjustable speed parameters.
  • Fail-Safe Battery Backup Kits: Adding compliant, monitored battery modules that automatically test their own holding charge and drive the door leaves open if power fails.
  • Electric Lock & Fire System Interfacing: Upgrading locking mechanisms to fail-secure or fail-safe configurations linked to the building’s central fire alarm matrix.
  • Safety Manifestation Decals: Applying compliant contrasting visual decals across glass panels to meet visual contrast standards.

A site assessment by an accredited technician will confirm whether your existing aluminum extrusions, tracks, and glass leaves are structurally sound enough to support a modern retrofit operator kit.

The Six-Month Service Requirement: What the Standard Mandates

Under Section 5 of AS5007, routine preventative maintenance is a mandatory component of compliance—not an optional recommendation. The standard states that powered pedestrian doors must undergo preventative maintenance inspections by a trained technician at intervals not exceeding six months.

What Happens During a Professional AS5007 Compliance Inspection?

  1. Sensor Walk-Testing: Testing the exact boundaries of the approach detection zone and threshold safety curtains using standard test dummies to verify no blind spots exist.
  2. Kinetic Energy & Impact Force Testing: Using specialized force gauges to verify that closing and opening forces do not exceed maximum statutory limits.
  3. Emergency Power Simulation: Isolating mains power to ensure the battery backup system activates immediately and drives the door to its designated failsafe position.
  4. Mechanical Hardware Inspection: Checking roller bearings, carriage brackets, floor guides, stops, and drive belts for mechanical play, wear, or structural degradation.
  5. Signage & Manifestation Audit: Confirming all visual safety markings and warning decals are intact, legible, and positioned at compliant heights.
  6. Logbook Endorsement: Recording all test results, component replacements, and safety calibrations in the building’s on-site service register for compliance auditing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does my existing automatic door need to be replaced to become AS5007 compliant?

Not necessarily. Many older doors can be retrofitted with updated sensors, battery backups, and modern drive controllers rather than being completely replaced. If the structural framing and glass are in good condition, a retrofit can bring the entire entrance up to current AS5007 and accessibility standards at a fraction of the cost of a full new installation.

Is AS5007 compliance mandatory for all commercial automatic doors in NSW?

Yes. AS5007 sets the definitive safety and performance standard for powered pedestrian doors throughout Australia. Compliance is required to satisfy general Building Code requirements, SafeWork NSW workplace safety obligations, and public liability duty of care provisions.

How do I know if my automatic door is currently compliant?

A professional inspection is the only reliable way to confirm compliance. An accredited technician will use calibrated force gauges and sensory test equipment to inspect sensor coverage, force limits, egress function, battery backup health, and safety signage against current AS5007 benchmarks.

How does AS5007 interface with NDIS and disability access laws?

While AS5007 focuses on the safe electromechanical operation of the door, it directly intersects with AS1428.1 (Design for access and mobility) and the Disability Discrimination Act (DDA). Together, they ensure that commercial automatic doors provide adequate clear opening widths, level thresholds, safe operational speeds, and reliable hands-free access for people with disabilities.

Can staff perform their own AS5007 compliance checks?

Building staff can perform basic daily operational checks—such as observing sensor lights, listening for unusual grinding noises, and verifying the door glides open smoothly. However, formal AS5007 compliance certification and logbook sign-offs must be carried out by a qualified, competent automatic door technician equipped with specialized testing tools.
